    Is the lidar telling the truth?

    Every safety check so far trusts the lidar: it stops for the dog, the drop and the steep bank because the lidar saw them. This project's first rule says the lidar is the sensor we believe. So we have to know when it's lying: a muddy window, water on the lens, rain, a driver that hangs. This post adds that check, and breaks the simulated lidar five ways to test it.

    Too steep

    The second ground hazard after drop-offs: a slope too steep to drive up, or to drive across without sliding. This post gives the robot a slope limit, two ways to see a steep bank coming, and a backstop for when it doesn't.

    The edge of the terrace

    A mower can hurt itself, or someone near it, without hitting anything. The worst way is driving off an edge: a retaining wall, a step, a pond bank. A fall can't be undone. This post gives the robot two separate ways to stay on top: the map, and its own eyes.

    Blade off on the paving

    A spinning blade on gravel or paving throws stones. Until now the robot avoided the problem by never leaving the lawn, so it couldn't cross a garden path, a patio or a driveway at all. That's the first of the three ground hazards in the perception plan (5.1c). The other two are drop-offs and slopes.

    Six robots, one brain

    Chris: "I bet I will come up with a few different hardware platforms. Two-wheel drive, four-wheel drive, tank drive, taller, shorter, wider." A review of the code found the robot's size written out in at least six places, two wheel joints hard-coded, sensing windows sized for one body, one blade assumed everywhere, and no way to tell the simulator which robot to run. So we fixed all of it the practical way: by designing five more robots and making the whole stack run each of them.

    Corners: stop short, turn, merge

    The first back-yard mow with the 3D lidar (backyard-raised-2) ended against the fence. On the outer lap, 35 cm from the lawn's edge, the robot drove right up to each corner before turning. That put its nose 14 cm past the edge, and at the fence the safety gate saw the fence inside its stop field and stopped the robot for good, 0.36 m away. Against a wall it would simply have hit it.
